# Writing style
- Clear and natural, academically serious, but easy for the author to understand and defend in class.
- Prefer direct verbs: use over utilise, help over facilitate when meanings align; retain technical vocabulary when needed.
- Vary sentence length naturally; avoid both chained subordinate clauses and monotonous short sentences.
- Avoid formulaic rhetorical reversals, especially 'not only ... but also' and 'not merely ... but rather'. Genuine comparisons remain permissible.
- Use transitions only when they convey a real relationship. Paragraphs do not require an explicit transition opener.
- Preserve specificity, hedging, and limitations. Never turn evidence-dependent tentative claims into categorical claims.
- Preserve the writer's voice. Their own examples, including Chinese cultural and fandom/digital archiving experience, should appear only when relevant to the assignment.
